Human-Moderated or AI-Moderated Interviews? How I Decide

I have run both on real product studies. Here are the four questions I ask before choosing, and a time I changed an AI-moderated plan to a human-moderated one.

The short answer

Use AI moderation when the questions are known and the job is consistent coverage across many people. Use a human moderator when the job is to find what you did not know to ask. Most studies sit somewhere between those two, so I decide with four questions.

The four questions I ask

  1. Do I already know the questions? A stable list of questions asked the same way to everyone suits an AI moderator. An exploratory study does not.
  2. Will I need to follow a thread nobody planned? Side conversations are where willingness to pay, workarounds, and emotional tone tend to surface.
  3. What is the stimulus? A prototype with a limited path, or a live conversation about a purchase decision, supports different kinds of moderation.
  4. How many people, and how fast? Twenty interviews in a few days is a different problem from seven sessions I can moderate myself.

When I chose AI-moderated

For a subscription study, I ran 20 AI-moderated interviews with purchasers, paired with a 105-person survey. The purpose was clear: understand how buyers decide on a multi-seat plan and which pricing mechanics they would accept. Every participant needed the same questions, and I needed the transcripts quickly. I read all of them. Buyers rejected promo codes and wanted seat-based licensing, so the team replaced the codes and repositioned the landing page.

When I switched to human-moderated

For a mobile travel prototype, the original brief proposed AI-moderated sessions. I changed it to human-moderated. Unmoderated sessions would have captured task completion and surface reactions, and they would have missed three things a task list cannot probe:

  • A cross-device pattern that appeared on its own, with participants planning on desktop and running the trip on mobile.
  • A willingness-to-pay signal that came up in a side conversation.
  • The anxious tone people used about flight warnings.

Those findings shaped the roadmap. A premium cross-device feature moved up, and the team had evidence for it. I also had every participant try two travel modes in counterbalanced order, which is much easier to run and adjust live with a person in the room.

What I check either way

  • Every quote in the readout matches the transcript word for word.
  • Participant counts on every slide match the data.
  • I read the transcripts myself. An AI summary is a starting point, not the finding.

The first two checks are covered in my post on AI quality checks.

Takeaway

The question is not whether AI moderation is good enough. It is whether the study is about verifying something you already understand or discovering something you do not. Choose the moderator that matches that job, and say why in your research plan.
