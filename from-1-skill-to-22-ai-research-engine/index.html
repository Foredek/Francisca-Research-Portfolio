From 1 Skill to 22: How I Built an AI Research Engine in One Week

I started the week with one custom AI skill and ended it with 22, three end-to-end research pipelines, two scheduled automations, and a live Slack monitoring workflow. One question did most of the work.

The habit that did it

At the end of every working session I asked one question: "Based on what we did today, what skills and automations could we build to make this workflow faster?" That question took me from 1 skill to 22 in a week. You do not need a playbook to start. You need to keep asking what you can build.

What I built

  • Study setup pipeline. I enter the study context once and get both a recruiting screener and a discussion guide.
  • Post-field closeout pipeline. It takes raw session materials to a synthesized findings document, a cross-study pattern check against my research repository, a confirmed executive summary, and a logged repository entry, all in one command.
  • Weekly planning package. It pulls my calendar, checks scope across active projects, confirms priorities, suggests time blocks, and preps any meeting I flag.

I also filled three real gaps: a screener generator, a discussion guide generator, and a cross-study synthesis tool. I had been doing all three by hand for every new study.

What it did on real work

I ran a full preference-study pipeline on a flagship product redesign. It parsed the survey export, generated the charts, built the complete readout deck, and ran QA and data accuracy checks. I also synthesized a prototype validation study across Google Drive, Confluence, and Slack into one Word document for the stakeholder readout.

What I had to calibrate

My Slack monitoring workflow surfaced two engagement opportunities that did not hold up. In the first, an engineering thread about "data" availability was about backend source data, not research findings. In the second, it flagged prep work that was already my job. I added a rule for each. I then added a third: when it is unclear whether to flag something, surface it and let me decide. More engagement is better than less, and passing is my call, not the tool's.

Time saved

  • Screener and discussion guide: 3 to 4 hours by hand, about 20 minutes with the pipeline including review.
  • Readout deck from a survey export: 6 to 8 hours across several sessions, about 2 hours end to end.
  • Weekly planning and meeting prep: 1 to 2 hours, about 20 to 30 minutes.
  • Rough total: 10 to 14 hours saved in one week, mostly on work that repeats.

Takeaways for researchers

  • At the end of any working session, ask what skills or automations you could build from what you just did.
  • AI tools need active calibration, not just prompting. When one gets something wrong, correct it precisely. The correction becomes a rule that makes every later run better.
