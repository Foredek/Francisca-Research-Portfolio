Research that ends in a decision

I am a UX and customer researcher with 10+ years of experience in healthcare, retail, consulting, and consumer apps. Most recently I led digital customer research at NAPA, and this year I completed three freelance studies for TWC.

I choose the method to fit the decision. That has meant diary studies, unmoderated A/B tests, AI-moderated interviews, surveys, and usability tests, often combined in one project.

I hold an MS.Ed in Applied Psychology from the University of Pennsylvania and a BBA from Emory University.

I believe the user should take center stage in their journey, with the product or service acting as a facilitator to help them achieve their objectives.

How I work

01

Frame the decision

I start with the decision the team has to make and the evidence it lacks, then write objectives around that.

02

Right-size the method

I pick the method that answers the question in the time available. I chose a diary study over usability testing for Snowcast, and I moved Multipack's second track to a survey when the prototype could not support testing.

03

Synthesize across sources

I code every response, compare themes across methods, and report what changes by context instead of giving one verdict.

04

Land it and scope the next study

I present recommendations the team can act on and name what to test next. The Radar readout led directly to a second study.
