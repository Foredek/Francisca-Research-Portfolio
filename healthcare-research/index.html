Peer-reviewed publications

Annals of the American Thoracic Society, 2021

Improving Prone Positioning for Severe ARDS during the COVID-19 Pandemic: An Implementation-Mapping Approach

My role: co-author. I contributed to data collection, analysis, and interpretation, and to writing and revising the manuscript.

Prone positioning saves lives in severe acute respiratory distress syndrome, yet most eligible patients do not receive it. The team interviewed 30 ICU clinicians across 12 ICUs at two health systems, then ran three focus groups with ICU leaders. We identified five determinants of use: knowledge, resources, alternative therapies, team culture, and patient factors. The task force then built five implementation strategies, and the health system rapidly put elements of them into practice during the COVID-19 response.

American Journal of Hospice and Palliative Medicine, 2021

Comparison of Web-Based and Paper Advance Directives: A Pilot Randomized Clinical Trial

My role: second author, at an academic palliative care research center.

Few patients with serious illness have an advance directive that clinicians can find in the medical record. We tested a web-based directive that records detailed care preferences, sends the finished plan to the electronic health record, and uses nudges to prompt sharing. Ninety-one patients with gastrointestinal and lung cancers were randomized. New documentation reached 28% of patients using the web version and 16% using paper, a gap too small to confirm in a pilot this size. Satisfaction was high in both groups, and 79% of web users shared their plans with caregivers, compared with 65% of paper users.

Studies in preparation
