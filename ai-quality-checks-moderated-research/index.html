Two AI Quality Checks Every Moderated Study Should Run

I delivered a full moderated study readout through my AI pipeline, and two automated checks caught errors I would not have found by hand under deadline pressure.

The study

This was a prototype validation study for a mobile product, with seven moderated sessions. The readout landed after nearly two weeks of synthesis, deck building, QA, and stakeholder alignment. The deck suite included insight slides, recommendations, and a section for each major feature area. The same week I posted a summary with the recording link so anyone who missed the call could catch up. The readout went well and the stakeholders left energized.

The two checks that earned their place

  • Quote fidelity check. It caught a quote I nearly used that was not verbatim in the session transcript. In research, a paraphrase presented as a quote can undo your credibility with a stakeholder.
  • Data accuracy preflight. It flagged a participant count overclaim on one slide. I would have presented a number the data did not support.

It was the first time I ran the post-session synthesis pipeline and recommendations generator at full capacity on a real study instead of a test run. Both checks took about 10 minutes. They catch what you stop seeing after your twelfth read of the deck.

Faster prep, cleaner handoffs

I prepped three meetings in one pass through my weekly planning skill, including a 1:1, an intake meeting, and a team sync. It took about 10 minutes instead of the 30 to 45 minutes it takes to pull context by hand across Slack and Drive. The follow-up study also became a formal research request, with a pre-populated brief that records the decisions already made, puts the biggest open question first, and states the timeline constraint up front so recruiting can start early.

Takeaway

If you run moderated studies and do not run a quote check and a data accuracy check before stakeholder delivery, add both. They are cheap, they are fast, and they protect the thing researchers sell, which is trust in the evidence.
